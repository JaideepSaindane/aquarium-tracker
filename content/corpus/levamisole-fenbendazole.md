Levamisole and fenbendazole are both effective, nematode-specific dewormers used for internal roundworm
infections like Camallanus and Capillaria (see corpus:internal-worms), but neither is always sold in a
dedicated aquarium formulation with a clear aquarium dose on the label — both are widely available as
livestock or veterinary products (for cattle, poultry, or other animals) dosed for a completely different
context and body of water.

This gap is the single biggest real-world risk with these medications: a keeper sourcing a livestock
dewormer and estimating an aquarium dose without a verified conversion is a well-documented and serious
overdose route in the hobby. Correct use means either finding a genuinely aquarium-formulated product with
its own clear dosing, or carefully following a verified, aquarium-specific conversion guide rather than
estimating.

Fenbendazole carries an additional, specific caution beyond dosing: it is toxic to snails and some other
invertebrate life even at doses that are otherwise appropriate for treating fish, so tanks with snail
populations worth keeping need either a different medication choice (levamisole, which is broadly
considered gentler on non-target invertebrates) or careful removal of snails before treatment.
