This group covers visible external parasites larger than protozoa: anchor worm (*Lernaea*, a thread-like
crustacean that burrows in and trails visible threads), fish lice (*Argulus*, a flat disc-shaped
crustacean that sits on the skin), and skin flukes (*Gyrodactylus*, generally too small to see individually
but causing flashing, excess mucus and patchy skin).

The most common mistake is treating a visible attached parasite as a simple mechanical problem — pull it
off, done. In practice, the visible worm or louse is only the adult stage of a life cycle that already has
free-swimming or egg stages elsewhere in the tank water, so removing the one visible parasite without
medicating the tank leaves the infestation intact and often leaves embedded mouthparts behind that become
an infection site.

Treatment needs to match the actual parasite group — praziquantel-based products for skin flukes, and a
medication specifically labelled for crustacean parasites for anchor worm and fish lice, since these are
biologically different parasite classes and a treatment effective against one does not reliably work
against the other. As with most external parasites, a repeat treatment several days to a week later is
typically needed to catch newly hatched individuals the first round missed.
