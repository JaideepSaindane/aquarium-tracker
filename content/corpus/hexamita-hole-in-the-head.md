Hole-in-the-head disease presents as small pits or eroded areas, typically starting around the eyes and
head and sometimes extending along the lateral line, most classically seen in large cichlids like oscars
and discus but reported in other species too. It is frequently confused with — and sometimes co-occurs
with — general lateral line erosion from poor water quality alone, which is part of why the exact cause is
genuinely debated in the hobby.

The protozoan parasite *Hexamita* is commonly implicated, but the hobby's own experience and the available
evidence point to water quality and nutrition as at least equally important contributing factors, and
possibly the primary drivers in many cases — the parasite may be present at low levels in many otherwise
healthy fish and only become a visible problem once the fish is stressed by poor water or a nutritionally
poor diet over an extended period.

This is why the correct first response is not automatically reaching for antibiotics or an antiparasitic —
it is reviewing and improving water quality and diet, which alone halts progression in many cases.
Medication (metronidazole, typically via medicated food since the parasite lives internally) is a
reasonable next step if improving water and diet alone does not stop the erosion, not the first move.
