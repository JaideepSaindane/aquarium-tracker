immediate_actions:
  - Float the sealed bag in the tank for about 15-20 minutes to equalise temperature gradually before opening it.
  - Gradually mix small amounts of tank water into the bag over 20-30+ minutes (drip acclimation for sensitive species, or repeated small additions for a simpler approach) rather than releasing fish directly into different water chemistry.
  - Never pour the original bag/transport water into the display tank — it may carry pathogens or stress hormones from the store or previous tank; net the fish out and discard the bag water.
  - Turn off tank lights and avoid excess handling/observation immediately after release, giving newly added fish time to settle with minimal added stress.
  - Quarantine new fish separately before adding to an established display tank whenever practical — see corpus:quarantine-setup, since acclimation and quarantine address different risks and both matter.
do_not_do:
  - Do not pour bag/transport water into the display tank — treat it as a biosecurity risk even when the fish look healthy.
  - Do not release fish into dramatically different water chemistry without any acclimation step, even for supposedly hardy species — a fast chemistry change is a real stressor regardless of species resilience.
  - Do not skip acclimation because a fish "seems fine" in the bag — internal stress from a fast chemistry shift is not always visibly obvious in the short term.

Acclimation addresses a narrower but real risk: a sudden, large difference between the water chemistry a
fish was transported in and the water it is about to be released into. Temperature is usually the more
familiar concern (floating the sealed bag), but the chemistry of the water itself — pH, hardness, dissolved
compounds — can differ just as meaningfully between a store's system and a home tank, which is why gradually
mixing tank water into the bag over time, not just floating it, is the more complete version of the process.

Bag water itself should never go into the display tank, independent of the acclimation process — it is
transport water from a shared retail system or another tank and carries a real, if hard-to-quantify,
pathogen and stress-hormone risk. Netting the fish out and discarding the bag water is a simple habit that
meaningfully reduces the chance of introducing something unwanted.

Acclimation and quarantine (corpus:quarantine-setup) are frequently conflated but address different risks —
acclimation is about avoiding an acute chemistry/temperature shock in the first hour, while quarantine is
about giving time for any disease incubating in a newly acquired fish to show itself before it reaches an
established display tank and its existing stock. A careful keeper does both, not one instead of the other.
