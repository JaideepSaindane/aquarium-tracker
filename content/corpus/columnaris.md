Columnaris (*Flavobacterium columnare*) is one of the fastest-killing common fish diseases, capable of
progressing from first visible signs to death within 24-72 hours — faster than ich, and comparable in
urgency to velvet. It is bacterial despite its classic "cotton wool" or "mouth fungus" appearance, which is
also its most consequential misdiagnosis trap: the cottony white patches look fungal, leading keepers to
reach for an antifungal treatment that does nothing against the actual bacterial cause while the disease
continues to progress on the fastest possible timeline in this corpus.

The second critical safety point is temperature. Several other common treatments in the hobby lean on
raising tank temperature — it speeds ich's vulnerable free-swimming stage, for instance — and this creates
a dangerous, easy-to-make mistake with columnaris specifically: raising the heat accelerates the bacteria's
own progression rather than helping fight it. The standard "warm the tank to help treat disease" reflex is
actively harmful here, and this is the single most important thing to get right when columnaris is
suspected.

Given the speed of this disease, treatment should begin as soon as it is suspected — correct identification
(bacterial, not fungal, cotton-wool-like patches, often starting at the mouth or as a saddle-shaped patch)
and prompt antibacterial treatment give a fish the best chance, and there is very little margin for a
"wait and see" approach that might be reasonable for a slower-moving condition.
