immediate_actions:
  - Use separate equipment (nets, siphons, algae scrapers, buckets) for different tanks wherever practical, especially between a quarantine/hospital tank and a healthy display tank.
  - Where separate equipment is not practical, rinse thoroughly and allow to fully dry between uses, or disinfect with a method appropriate for aquarium equipment, before moving equipment between tanks.
  - Avoid moving water, substrate, or decor directly between tanks without considering what could travel with it, particularly from a tank with any known or suspected health issue.
  - Wash hands between handling different tanks, particularly after handling a tank known or suspected to have a health issue, before touching another tank.
  - Treat any equipment that has touched a quarantine or hospital tank as needing extra care (dedicated use, or thorough disinfection) before it touches a healthy display tank.
do_not_do:
  - Do not assume equipment is automatically safe to move between tanks just because it looks clean — many relevant pathogens and parasites are not visible to the naked eye.
  - Do not share equipment between a quarantine/hospital tank and a healthy display tank without disinfecting between uses — this is one of the more common, avoidable ways an isolated problem spreads to an otherwise healthy tank.
  - Do not treat this topic as only relevant to keepers with visibly sick fish — good equipment hygiene is a preventive habit worth maintaining across a multi-tank setup generally, not just during an active disease event.

Shared equipment is an easy-to-overlook disease vector precisely because it does not look like one — a net
or siphon appears clean, but many of the parasites and pathogens covered elsewhere in this corpus (flukes,
protozoa, various bacteria) are microscopic and can travel on equipment that shows no visible sign of
carrying anything. Anyone keeping more than one tank, or maintaining a quarantine setup alongside a display
tank, benefits from treating equipment hygiene as a routine habit rather than something to think about only
once a problem has already appeared.

The highest-value habit for most home setups is simple: dedicate specific equipment to a quarantine or
hospital tank and never let it touch a healthy display tank without thorough cleaning and drying, or proper
disinfection, in between. This single practice closes off one of the more common, entirely avoidable paths
by which an isolated health issue in one tank ends up in another.

This is a genuinely preventive, low-drama topic — there is no symptom to watch for, only a habit worth
building — but it connects directly to why quarantine (corpus:quarantine-setup) works as a strategy in the
first place: isolating a new or sick fish only protects the rest of a collection if the equipment moving
between tanks is not quietly undoing that isolation.
