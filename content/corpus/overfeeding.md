immediate_actions:
  - Reduce feeding to what fish visibly finish within about 2-3 minutes, once or twice a day for most community fish species.
  - Remove any uneaten food after a few minutes rather than leaving it to decompose in the tank.
  - If water quality issues are already present from a period of overfeeding, address them directly (increased water changes) while also correcting the feeding habit going forward.
  - Recognise that most adult fish tolerate occasional missed feeding days without harm — err toward slightly underfeeding rather than overfeeding when uncertain.

  - Do not feed based on how eager fish appear to eat — most fish will beg for and accept food well beyond what they actually need, and appetite alone is not a reliable guide to correct portion size.

  - Do not feed multiple times a day "to be safe" without checking that each feeding is actually being fully consumed within a couple of minutes.

Overfeeding is a quiet, cumulative root cause behind a large share of "my fish is sick" reports that never
get traced back to it, because its effects — elevated ammonia and nitrate, cloudy water, occasional
swim-bladder issues — look like separate, unrelated problems rather than symptoms of the same single habit.

The core misunderstanding is that fish's visible eagerness to eat is not a reliable signal of how much they
actually need. Most species will accept and beg for food well past their nutritional requirement, so
feeding based on appetite rather than a fixed, modest portion consistently overshoots. The practical rule —
feed only what is fully consumed within about two to three minutes, once or twice a day for most community
species — exists specifically to counter this mismatch between appetite and actual need.

Uneaten food left in the tank compounds the problem beyond the fish's own consumption: it decomposes and
adds directly to the ammonia and nitrate load whether or not it is eventually eaten, which is why removing
visible leftover food matters as much as controlling the portion in the first place. When genuinely
uncertain how much to feed, erring toward slightly less rather than more is the safer default — most adult
fish tolerate an occasional light or skipped feeding without any real harm.
