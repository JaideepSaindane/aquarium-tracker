True fungus, caused by *Saprolegnia* and related water moulds, is genuinely fungal — unlike columnaris,
which despite its "cotton wool" and "mouth fungus" nicknames is bacterial. Telling the two apart correctly
matters because they need entirely different medication classes, and treating one as the other wastes
critical time, especially given how much faster columnaris can kill.

The clearest practical differentiator is what the growth is on and where it started. True fungus is
largely opportunistic — it takes hold on tissue that is already damaged, dead, or otherwise compromised
(an existing wound, dead tissue, unfertilised fish eggs) rather than attacking healthy, intact skin
directly, and it tends to progress more slowly than columnaris. Columnaris more classically starts around
the mouth or as a saddle-shaped patch on the back and progresses fast enough to be lethal within days.

Practically, if the growth is clearly associated with a prior injury and progressing at a normal, slower
pace, true fungus with an appropriate antifungal treatment is the reasonable presumptive diagnosis. If
there is genuine uncertainty, or the growth started at the mouth or is moving fast, treating presumptively
for columnaris first is the safer default given the difference in how much time each condition allows
before it becomes fatal.
