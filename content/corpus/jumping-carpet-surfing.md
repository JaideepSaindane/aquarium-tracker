Jumping — a fish clearing the water and ending up outside the tank, often found too late to save — is
entirely preventable and yet remains a common, painful loss for keepers who did not realise a specific
species needed a secure lid, or who assumed their existing lid was actually gap-free.

The practical reality of most real jumping incidents is that the tank did have a lid — the fish escaped
through a gap around an equipment cutout, a cord entry point, or a corner seam, not through a fully open
top. This is why "does my tank have a lid" is the wrong question; the right question is whether every gap
in that lid is small enough to actually stop the specific fish in the tank.

Certain groups are well known in the hobby for a strong jumping tendency — many killifish and hatchetfish
particularly, along with some tetras and danios — and for these species a secure, gap-free lid is a hard
requirement to research and confirm before stocking, not an optional nicety. Even normally calm species can
jump when startled by a sudden fright, so a secure lid is good general practice regardless of the specific
species involved, and keeping the water level a sensible distance below the rim reduces the risk further
for anything prone to jumping.
