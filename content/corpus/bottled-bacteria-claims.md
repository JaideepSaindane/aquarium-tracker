Bottled "starter bacteria" or "instant cycle" products claim to seed a new tank with live nitrifying
bacteria, shortcutting the weeks a cycle normally takes. The hobby's experience with these products is
genuinely mixed — some contain viable bacteria and produce a real speed-up under the right conditions,
others contain bacterial strains that do not survive well in aquarium conditions or work far more slowly
than advertised, and product quality has historically varied between brands and even between batches.

The practical risk is not the product itself so much as what it encourages: users skip testing and fully
stock a tank on day one because the label promised an instant cycle, then discover — too late — that the
promised speed-up did not happen as advertised. Confirmed, reliably effective seeding uses biological
material from an already-established, healthy tank (filter media, substrate, a sponge) rather than a
bottled product, because that is transplanting a proven colony rather than trusting a shelf-stored one.

The honest position on these products: they may help, but they are not a substitute for testing, and they
should not change the underlying advice to add fish gradually and confirm the tank can actually process
ammonia before relying on it to do so.
