immediate_actions:
  - Set up the tank fully first — substrate, decor, filter, heater running, water dechlorinated — before adding any ammonia source.
  - Add a source of ammonia with no fish present: pure ammonia solution (no surfactants/perfumes), a small amount of fish food left to rot, or seeded filter media/substrate from an established, disease-free tank to speed things up dramatically.
  - Dose to roughly 2-4 ppm ammonia and test daily. Track ammonia, then nitrite, then nitrate as the cycle progresses — ammonia will fall first as the first bacteria establish, then nitrite will rise and later fall as the second group establishes.
  - Keep redosing ammonia to maintain roughly 2-4 ppm each time it drops to near 0, to keep feeding the establishing bacteria.
  - The tank is cycled when it can process a full dose of ammonia down to 0 ammonia and 0 nitrite within about 24 hours, with nitrate present.
  - Once cycled, do a large water change to bring nitrate down to a safe starting level before adding fish, and add fish gradually rather than fully stocking on day one.
do_not_do:
  - Do not add fish before the tank has demonstrated it can process a full ammonia dose to 0/0 (ammonia/nitrite) within 24 hours — an incompletely cycled tank exposes fish to exactly the ammonia and nitrite spikes this whole process exists to avoid.
  - Do not use an ammonia source that contains surfactants, perfumes or additives — check the label specifically says "pure" ammonia with no other ingredients.
  - Do not skip testing and just wait "a few weeks" — cycling time varies significantly by tank, and only testing tells you it is actually done.
  - Do not forget the final water change before adding fish — a freshly cycled tank often has high nitrate from the dosing process, which needs diluting before fish go in.

Fishless cycling is the safe default way to establish a tank's biofilter: feed ammonia to the tank
deliberately, with no livestock present, until the bacteria that convert ammonia to nitrite and nitrite to
nitrate have grown enough to process it quickly. Nothing living is exposed to the toxic intermediate
stages this way — see corpus:nitrogen-cycle for how the process itself works.

The practical method is straightforward: dose pure ammonia (no surfactants or perfume additives) to around
2-4 ppm, test daily, and keep redosing as the reading falls toward zero. Ammonia will drop first as the
first bacterial population establishes; nitrite will then rise and later fall as the second population
catches up. The tank is ready when a full dose of ammonia clears to zero ammonia and zero nitrite within
about 24 hours.

Seeding the tank with filter media, substrate, or a sponge from an already-established, healthy tank
dramatically shortens this process — sometimes to days rather than weeks — because it transplants an
existing bacterial colony rather than growing one from nothing.

This is the method most consistently recommended by the hobby as the lowest-risk way to set up a new tank,
in contrast to fish-in cycling (corpus:fish-in-cycling), which exposes real animals to the toxic stages and
should be treated as a damage-limitation situation rather than a first choice.
