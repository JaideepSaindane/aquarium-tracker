Popeye — visible bulging of one or both eyes — is a symptom with two quite different likely explanations
depending on a single distinguishing detail: whether it affects one eye or both. This distinction matters
more than almost anything else about the case, and should be the first question asked.

A single bulging eye (unilateral) is most often straightforward physical injury — the fish struck
something, or was struck by a tankmate — causing local fluid buildup. These cases frequently resolve on
their own with nothing more than clean water to prevent a secondary infection taking hold at the injury
site, and do not typically need medication.

Both eyes bulging (bilateral) points toward something systemic rather than a local injury — a bacterial
infection, poor water quality affecting the whole fish, or in rarer cases dissolved gas supersaturation
from an equipment issue. Bilateral popeye deserves the same level of concern as other systemic-illness
signs, and can appear alongside or as an early stage of dropsy (corpus:dropsy), so a broader review of the
fish's overall condition and the tank's water quality is warranted rather than treating the eyes in
isolation.
