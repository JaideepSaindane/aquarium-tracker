Gill flukes (*Dactylogyrus* and related monogenean parasites) live on and damage gill tissue directly, and
unlike ich or velvet, they are essentially invisible without a microscope — there is no external spot or
coating to look for. This makes them one of the most commonly misdiagnosed causes of laboured breathing:
a keeper tests ammonia, nitrite, nitrate and temperature, finds everything normal, and concludes the fish
must be fine, when the actual cause is a parasite that will never show up on a water test.

The practical diagnostic pattern is exactly this combination — rapid or laboured breathing, clamped or
flared gills, flashing, with genuinely normal water parameters. Because home aquarists cannot realistically
do a gill scrape and microscope examination, presumptive treatment based on this symptom pattern is the
standard, accepted approach rather than waiting for definitive confirmation.

Treatment needs a medication specifically effective against monogenean flukes — praziquantel-based products
are the standard choice — rather than a generic broad-spectrum treatment, many of which simply do not work
against this parasite class. A repeat dose several days later is typically needed to catch eggs that
survive the first treatment and hatch afterward.
