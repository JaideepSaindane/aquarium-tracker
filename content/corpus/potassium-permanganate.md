Potassium permanganate is cheap, widely available across India, and has a long history of informal use in
the hobby — which makes it easy to underestimate how unforgiving it actually is. The gap between a dose
that is effective against external parasites and a dose that burns gill tissue and kills the fish is
narrow, narrower than most other medications covered in this corpus, and that margin shrinks further and
less predictably in the presence of organic matter, which rapidly inactivates the chemical.

This combination — narrow safety margin plus unpredictable inactivation by organics — is why it should be
used only as a short, controlled dip in a separate clean container following a verified aquarium-specific
protocol, never as a sustained treatment dosed directly into a display tank with substrate, decor and
organic waste present. A dose calculated for clean dip water and a dose that would be needed in an organic-
laden display tank are not comparable, and guessing at the difference is exactly where this chemical
becomes dangerous.

Its low cost and long history of hobby use should not be read as evidence of it being a gentle or
forgiving option — if anything, the opposite is true relative to most alternatives in this corpus.
