Antibiotics are freely available in India without a prescription or diagnosis for aquarium use, which
removes the gatekeeping that exists in some other markets and puts real responsibility on the individual
keeper's judgement about when they are actually warranted. This corpus takes the position that antibiotics
are a targeted response to a confirmed or strongly presumptive bacterial infection — not a general "my fish
looks a bit off, let's try antibiotics" first response, for two concrete reasons beyond the general
principle of not overusing antibiotics.

First, antibiotics do not distinguish between the specific pathogen being targeted and the beneficial
nitrifying bacteria that make up a tank's biofilter. A course of antibiotics dosed into a display tank can
genuinely crash the cycle, converting a disease problem into an ammonia problem on top of it (see
corpus:cycle-crash). Second, incomplete courses — stopping early because the fish looks better — are a
well-documented way resistant bacterial strains develop, which is a real cost both to that tank's future
treatment options and, more broadly, to antibiotic effectiveness generally.

The practical guidance: reserve antibiotics for cases with a clear bacterial signature (advancing fin rot
not responding to water changes, visible ulcers or systemic reddening, columnaris) rather than as a default
reach, correct water quality alongside treatment since it is usually the underlying enabler, and always
complete the full course once started.
