Prophylactic treatment during quarantine — medicating a fish that shows no symptoms, as a preventive measure
against something that might be incubating — is a real practice in the hobby but a genuinely contested one,
not a settled best practice with a single correct answer. Reasonable, experienced keepers land on different
sides of it, and the right choice depends heavily on the specific source, species, and known regional
disease risk involved, which is exactly why this entry does not prescribe a single default protocol.

The clearest, least contested risk within this topic is medication stacking (corpus:medication-stacking) —
applying multiple medications together prophylactically "to be thorough" compounds interaction risk and
physiological stress in a way that has genuinely killed entire quarantine batches, turning a preventive
measure into the very harm it was meant to avoid. Any prophylactic approach should stay narrow and
deliberate, grounded in a specific, understood risk, rather than broad and precautionary.

Because this decision involves weighing a real but unconfirmed risk against real medication exposure to an
otherwise healthy fish, and because several relevant medications carry their own species-specific dangers
(see the individual medication entries in this corpus), this is a topic worth a real veterinary or
aquaculture professional's input rather than a confident, self-directed default — reflected here by this
entry's Tier 1 review requirement.
