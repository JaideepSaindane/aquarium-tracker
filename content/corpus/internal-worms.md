Internal worms, most commonly *Camallanus* (a nematode that is visibly distinctive — thin, red or pink
worms protruding from the fish's vent) and *Capillaria* (usually inferred only from weight loss and
stringy faeces rather than seen directly), are a common but frequently mishandled parasite group. Because
Camallanus is visually alarming, it tends to panic keepers into folk remedies — salt, heat — that have no
real effect on an internal nematode parasite living in the gut.

The correct treatment is a proper dewormer — levamisole or fenbendazole-based products are the standard,
effective choices — dosed for the whole tank, since other fish are very likely exposed even without visible
symptoms yet. A single treatment is rarely sufficient on its own: these parasites' life cycles typically
include eggs or larvae already present in the substrate or water that will continue to hatch after the
first dose, so a repeat treatment per the product's own instructions is standard practice, not overkill.

Fenbendazole specifically is worth flagging: it is broadly effective against nematodes, but it is also
harmful to snails and some other invertebrate life, so tanks with wanted snail populations need either a
different medication choice or careful protection of the snails during treatment.
