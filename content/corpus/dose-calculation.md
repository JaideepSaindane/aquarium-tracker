Every medication dose in this corpus, and every product label in the hobby generally, is calculated
against the tank's actual water volume — not its nominal or box size. This distinction sounds obvious
stated plainly, but in practice it is one of the most common and consequential real-world sources of
medication overdose, because most keepers dose based on the size printed on the tank's box or listing
rather than the true volume of water actually in it.

Substrate, decor, hardscape, equipment, and an unfilled water line (most tanks are not filled brim-full)
all displace water, and the combined effect routinely reduces true water volume by 20-35% compared to the
tank's advertised size. A medication dosed for a "200 litre tank" that actually holds 140 litres of water
after substrate and decor is effectively dosed around 40% over the intended concentration — a meaningful
overdose from a calculation error, not a mistake in the product itself.

The practical fix is straightforward: calculate real water volume from actual dimensions and fill level,
adjusted down for substrate and large decor, before dosing anything — and when genuinely uncertain, err
toward a volume estimate that slightly overstates rather than understates true water, since this leads to
underdosing (usually just a slower or weaker effect) rather than the more dangerous overdose direction.
