Praziquantel is a genuinely safe and effective medication for what it actually treats — monogenean flukes
(both gill and skin flukes) and some tapeworm infections — with a wider margin of safety than most other
medications in this corpus. This safety and effectiveness has, somewhat ironically, led to it being
over-applied as a general "parasite treatment" for conditions it does not actually touch.

Praziquantel does nothing meaningful against protozoan parasites like ich or velvet, nematodes/roundworms
(which need levamisole or fenbendazole instead, see corpus:levamisole-fenbendazole), or crustacean
parasites like anchor worm and fish lice (which need a crustacean-specific treatment, see
corpus:skin-flukes-and-crustacean-parasites). Using it for the wrong parasite class produces no visible
side effect — because it is safe — but also produces no result, which can waste critical treatment time on
a condition where waiting matters.

The correct use pattern is an initial dose followed by a repeat treatment roughly 5-7 days later, to catch
parasite eggs or newly hatched individuals the first dose could not reach — a single treatment alone often
leaves the parasite's life cycle intact.
