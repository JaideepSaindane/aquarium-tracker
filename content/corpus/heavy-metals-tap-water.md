symptoms:
  - Invertebrates (shrimp, snails) dying rapidly with no other explanation, often after new plumbing work, a new building, or water sitting in pipes overnight
  - Fish showing acute distress, gasping, or unusual colouration after a water change specifically using "first draw" water (the first water out of a tap after it has sat unused for hours)

likely_causes:
  - Copper or brass plumbing leaching copper into water that has sat stagnant in the pipes for hours (overnight, or after a long gap) — "first draw" water
  - New or recently renovated plumbing, which leaches more heavily in the first weeks to months
  - RO/purifier "reject" or concentrate water mistakenly used for the tank instead of the product (permeate) water — reject water concentrates whatever was filtered out, including metals
  - Old or corroding pipes in general, particularly in older buildings
  - Copper-based medication residue from another tank's equipment (nets, buckets) shared with an invertebrate tank
immediate_actions:
  - Stop using "first draw" water for tank top-offs and water changes — run the tap for a minute or two before collecting water, to flush out water that has been sitting in the pipes.
  - If you suspect an active poisoning event, do a water change immediately with water that has been run/flushed first, and increase aeration.
  - Use a dechlorinator/conditioner that also detoxifies heavy metals if available — many modern multi-purpose conditioners include this.
  - If using an RO/purifier system, confirm which output line is actually connected to the tank — the product (permeate) line, not the reject/concentrate line.
  - Keep shrimp/invertebrate-only equipment (nets, buckets, tools) entirely separate from equipment that has ever touched a copper-medicated tank.
do_not_do:
  - Do not use water straight from a tap that has not been run for at least a minute if the building's plumbing includes copper or brass fittings, especially for shrimp or snail tanks.
  - Do not assume an RO/purifier system is automatically safe for tank use without confirming which output line is being used — reject water is the opposite of safe.
  - Do not reuse nets, buckets or tools between a copper-medicated tank and any tank containing shrimp, snails, or other invertebrates, even after rinsing — copper residue is persistent.

  - New construction or renovation nearby that may have disturbed old pipes — worth extra caution and testing for a period afterward.

Copper is lethal to invertebrates — shrimp and snails — at concentrations far below anything that
noticeably affects most fish, which is exactly why it is dangerous: a copper contamination event can wipe
out a shrimp colony while leaving fish in the same tank looking completely normal, leading keepers to rule
out water quality as the cause entirely.

The most common real-world source is not the water supply itself but the plumbing it travels through.
Copper and brass fittings leach measurably more copper into water that has been sitting stagnant in the
pipe for hours — "first draw" water, typically the first water out of a tap in the morning or after any
long gap. Running the tap for a minute or two before collecting water flushes this out and is the single
most effective, free prevention step.

A second, India-relevant source is RO/purifier plumbing confusion: these systems produce two streams, a
clean product (permeate) line and a concentrated reject line that carries away whatever was filtered out,
including metals. Using the wrong line for the tank is an easy mistake with a serious consequence for
sensitive invertebrates.

Because general aquarium test kits do not test for copper or other heavy metals, this cause is easy to
miss entirely — it is worth deliberately ruling out with a dedicated metals test kit whenever invertebrate
deaths have no other explanation.
