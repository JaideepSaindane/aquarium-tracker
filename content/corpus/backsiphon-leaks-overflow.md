Back-siphoning is a specific, preventable mechanism worth understanding on its own: when a filter or pump
loses power (during a power cut, see corpus:power-cuts, or simply when unplugged for maintenance), any
tubing that runs below the tank's waterline without a check valve can let water drain backward out of the
tank rather than staying in place — sometimes losing a surprising volume before anyone notices, particularly
overnight. A simple check valve fitted to any such line is a low-cost, permanent fix once the mechanism is
understood.

Leaks and overflows more broadly share a common practical lesson: fixing the visible mess (water on the
floor) without identifying and correcting the actual cause — a loose fitting, an overfilled level, a missing
check valve — just sets up a repeat of the same event. The cause always deserves attention before the
equipment goes back into normal operation.

When a significant volume of water has been lost, replacing it carefully — dechlorinated and roughly
temperature-matched — avoids turning an equipment problem into a second, parameter-related stress on top of
whatever the fish already experienced during the event itself.
