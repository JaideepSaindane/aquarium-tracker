Overstocking is a root cause that quietly drives a large share of the other problems in this corpus —
nitrate creep, aggression, chronic poor water quality — without necessarily presenting as a distinct problem
of its own. It is also a genuinely common trap in the Indian retail context specifically, where advice at
the point of sale routinely understates adult size and true space requirements, and images of a single
goldfish in a small bowl remain a widely circulated (and biologically inappropriate) picture of what
"normal" fishkeeping looks like.

The core mistake is buying and stocking based on a fish's size at purchase rather than its adult size and
real space/social needs — many popular species sold small grow substantially larger, or need larger groups
or more swimming space than their juvenile size suggests. Overstocking then compounds gradually: fish added
incrementally over time, each individually reasonable, without anyone recalculating the tank's total bioload
against its actual capacity as the stock grows.

There is no single reliable formula (the popular "inch per gallon" rule is a rough, frequently misleading
oversimplification) — the right approach is researching each specific species' adult size, social needs,
and bioload before buying, and treating that research as the actual stocking decision rather than an
afterthought once fish are already home.
