Methylene blue is a genuinely useful medication for a narrow set of purposes — most notably treating
fungal-infected fish eggs to protect the viable ones nearby, and as a hospital-tank or dip treatment for
early fungal issues or certain protozoal support — but it is a poor fit for use in a display tank with an
established biological filter, since it is known to harm or strip nitrifying bacteria. Using it there risks
converting a fungal problem into an ammonia problem.

It also stains everything it touches — silicone seals, plastic decor, and plant tissue — often permanently
on porous surfaces, which is a purely cosmetic but genuinely irreversible consequence worth knowing before
using it in any tank a keeper cares about the appearance of.

The practical rule: methylene blue belongs in a dedicated hospital tank or as a short dip/egg treatment, not
as a general-purpose or precautionary dose in a planted or biologically mature display tank.
