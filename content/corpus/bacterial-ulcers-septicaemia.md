Bacterial ulcers and haemorrhagic septicaemia describe a serious, systemic bacterial infection — visible as
open sores, red streaking under the skin (especially around fin bases and the belly), or diffuse
reddening — that is a step beyond a localised problem like simple fin rot. This is a whole-body infection,
and it needs to be recognised and treated as such rather than approached with the more cautious,
water-quality-first sequence appropriate for milder conditions.

The practical mistake this entry exists to prevent is treating visible red streaking or open sores with
only an external dip or topical spot treatment. Systemic infection needs a systemic treatment approach —
internal antibiotics, often delivered via medicated food since the infection is not confined to the
surface, or a genuinely systemic water-column antibacterial. Waiting to see if a milder, external-only
treatment works first wastes time this condition does not generously provide.

As with most bacterial disease in this corpus, chronic poor water quality is the most common underlying
contributor, weakening the fish's defences enough for an opportunistic infection to take hold and spread —
correcting water quality alongside prompt medical treatment addresses both the immediate infection and the
conditions that allowed it to become this severe.
