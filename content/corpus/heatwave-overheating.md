symptoms:
  - Water temperature reading well above the species' normal safe range, commonly above 32-34°C during Indian summers in unchilled/unshaded tanks

likely_causes:
  - Ambient room temperature during peak Indian summer months pushing an unchilled tank well past a safe range, especially in direct sun or a poorly ventilated room
  - No cooling measures (fan, AC in the room, chiller) in place for species that need one during the hottest months
  - A tank located near a window, in direct sunlight, or near another heat source (electronics, an unventilated wall)
immediate_actions:
  - Increase surface agitation and aeration immediately — this is the fastest way to improve available oxygen while working on cooling the water itself.
  - Add a fan blowing across the water surface — evaporative cooling from this is a genuinely effective, low-cost, immediate intervention and often the fastest way to bring temperature down measurably within hours.
  - Float a sealed bag or bottle of ice/frozen water in the tank if temperature is critically high — sealed, so no direct contact between ice/cold water and tank water, and done gradually rather than dropping temperature too fast.
  - Move the tank out of direct sunlight if that is a contributing factor, and improve room ventilation where possible.
  - Reduce feeding — less food means less metabolic/waste load at a time when the fish's system is already stressed.
do_not_do:
  - Do not add ice or very cold water directly into the tank water — this causes a rapid, localised temperature swing that is its own stressor on top of the heat itself; use a sealed container instead, and cool gradually.
  - Do not ignore a gasping fish in a heatwave as "just the heat, nothing to be done" — active cooling intervention (fan, aeration, shade) genuinely helps and should be applied promptly, not treated as an unavoidable seasonal inconvenience.
  - Do not keep cool-water species (goldfish, White Clouds, most hillstream loaches) in a location with no cooling plan for the hottest months — this needs planning ahead of summer, not reacting during a heatwave.

Indian summers routinely push unchilled, unshaded aquariums well past safe temperature ranges — 34°C and
above is not unusual in a room without cooling during the hottest months — and this is dangerous for two
compounding reasons at once: warmer water holds measurably less dissolved oxygen, at exactly the same time
that a fish's metabolic rate and oxygen demand are rising with the heat. This double bind is why heat stress
kills quickly relative to some other environmental problems on this list.

The good news is that effective cooling does not require expensive equipment as a first response. A fan
blowing across the open water surface drives real evaporative cooling and is often the fastest, cheapest,
most accessible intervention — genuinely capable of bringing temperature down by a meaningful margin within
hours. Increased surface agitation and aeration helps independently by improving available oxygen even
before temperature itself comes down.

For an acute spike, a sealed container of ice or frozen water floated in the tank provides more active
cooling, but the sealing and gradual approach matter — direct ice or very cold water added to the tank
causes its own rapid, localised temperature swing, trading one stressor for another. For anyone keeping
cool-water species in a hot climate long-term, planning proper cooling (a chiller, or reliable room AC)
ahead of summer is the more durable fix than repeated emergency measures each heatwave.
