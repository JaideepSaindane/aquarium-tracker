Dropsy is not itself a disease — it is a sign, almost always of serious internal organ failure (most
commonly kidney failure from a systemic bacterial infection) causing fluid to build up in the body cavity
and, in advanced cases, the scales to protrude outward in the distinctive "pinecone" appearance that gives
the condition its common nickname among keepers.

This corpus takes an honesty-first position on prognosis: by the time scales are visibly protruding,
significant internal organ damage has usually already occurred, and reported treatment success at that
stage is genuinely low across the hobby literature and veterinary sources. This is not a reason to
discourage trying — early intervention before pineconing, when only mild swelling is present, has a better
outlook — but a user asking about visible pineconing deserves an honest answer about the odds, not false
reassurance that delays a difficult but sometimes kinder decision.

Supportive treatment combines an antibacterial medication (addressing the likely underlying infection) with
an Epsom salt bath (magnesium sulfate, not the same as regular aquarium salt), which helps reduce fluid
retention through osmosis. Where a fish is clearly suffering with an advanced, non-responding case, humane
euthanasia is a legitimate and sometimes the most responsible option, and this corpus treats that
conversation as part of giving complete, honest guidance rather than something to avoid mentioning.
