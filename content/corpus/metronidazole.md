Metronidazole is the standard treatment for internal protozoal parasites — most notably Hexamita, relevant
to hole-in-the-head disease (corpus:hexamita-hole-in-the-head) — and its effectiveness depends heavily on
delivery method in a way that is easy to get wrong. Because the target parasite lives internally, in the
gut, simply dissolving the medication in the tank water and hoping the fish absorbs enough through its
gills or skin is considerably less reliable than delivering it directly via medicated food, where the fish
actually ingests a controlled, effective dose.

This is a common and consequential gap in how the medication gets used in practice: a keeper doses the
water column, sees no improvement, and concludes the medication does not work or the diagnosis was wrong,
when the more likely explanation is that water dosing alone was never the most effective delivery route for
this particular condition.

Medicated food is the preferred method whenever the fish is still eating. Water-column dosing remains a
reasonable fallback for a fish that has stopped eating, understood honestly as a less reliable option for
this specific use case rather than an equally good alternative.
