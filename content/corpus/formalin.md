Formalin (a formaldehyde solution) is an effective treatment against several external parasites, often
combined with malachite green in commercial ich and parasite treatments, but it carries two serious risks
that set it apart from most other medications in this corpus: it consumes dissolved oxygen significantly,
compounding respiratory stress in fish that may already be struggling to breathe, and it is a genuine human
health hazard through vapour inhalation and skin/eye contact during handling.

The oxygen-depletion effect matters most in a tank where fish are already gasping or stressed — adding a
treatment that further reduces available oxygen at exactly that moment can do real harm on its own,
independent of the medication's intended effect on the parasite. Heavy aeration during and after treatment
is not optional supportive care here; it is a necessary part of using this medication safely.

The human-health dimension deserves equal weight, particularly relevant given how many aquariums in India
are kept in small, less-ventilated rooms: formaldehyde vapour is a genuine respiratory irritant and hazard,
and formalin should be handled with the same care as any hazardous household chemical — good ventilation,
avoided skin/eye contact, and careful, deliberate dosing rather than "a bit extra to be sure."
