symptoms:
  - Shrimp or soft-water fish dying or showing distress shortly after a water change, with no ammonia/nitrite/nitrate explanation
  - Shrimp found dead shortly after moulting, or "dead in shell"
  - Fish or shrimp appearing lethargic, floating oddly, or struggling to swim normally after fresh water is added

likely_causes:
  - Water source that varies significantly between fills — common with tanker-delivered water, borewell water, or RO/purifier "reject" water in many Indian cities, where mineral content is inconsistent day to day
  - Switching water sources (e.g. from tap to RO, or between different tanker suppliers) without checking how different the new source's TDS/GH/KH actually is
  - Topping off evaporated water with a different source than what the tank was originally filled and maintained with
  - RO or purified water used directly with no remineralisation, causing an unstable, mineral-poor environment
immediate_actions:
  - Test TDS (or GH/KH if no TDS meter), of both the tank water and the water about to be added, before doing a water change — do not assume they match just because both are "tap water" or both are "RO water".
  - If there is a significant mismatch, do a smaller water change than usual (10-15%) and space subsequent changes closer together rather than doing the normal full change in one go.
  - For shrimp specifically, treat any large TDS/GH/KH swing as a real risk, even if fish in the same tank show no symptoms — shrimp are considerably more sensitive to mineral swings than most fish.
  - If using RO or purified water, remineralise it to a stable target GH/KH before adding it to the tank, rather than adding it plain.
  - Standardise on one water source and one remineralisation routine going forward rather than switching between sources tank to tank or week to week.
do_not_do:
  - Do not assume "clean" or "pure" water is automatically safer for a direct large water change — RO/purified water with zero mineral content is itself a shock to livestock adapted to a mineral-bearing tank, particularly shrimp.
  - Do not mix water sources for a single water change without testing both first, especially with tanker or borewell water where mineral content genuinely varies fill to fill.
  - Do not assume that because fish in a mixed-stock tank look fine, shrimp in the same tank are also fine — shrimp tolerate mineral swings far worse.

  - A water source that is known to vary significantly and cannot be made consistent (e.g. shared building tanker supply) — worth considering a dedicated, tested top-off/change reservoir rather than drawing straight from an unpredictable source each time.

TDS (total dissolved solids), general hardness (GH) and carbonate hardness (KH) describe the mineral
content of water, and livestock — shrimp especially — acclimate physiologically to whatever level their
tank has stabilised at. The danger is not a high or low number in isolation; it is a large, sudden
difference between the tank's water and whatever is added during a water change or top-off.

This matters more in India than in many other markets because water sources are genuinely inconsistent:
tanker-delivered water varies by supplier and even by fill, borewell water can shift seasonally, and RO or
purifier "reject" water used for topping off carries essentially no mineral content at all — each of these
represents a real risk of a sudden GH/KH/TDS swing if used without checking it against what the tank has
been running on.

Shrimp are the most sensitive common livestock to this — a large mismatch during a water change is a
well-documented cause of post-change deaths and failed moults, often blamed on disease when the actual
cause was a mineral swing. The fix is simple in principle: test both the tank and the incoming water before
a change, keep the difference small, and if using RO or purified water, remineralise it to a stable target
rather than adding it plain.
