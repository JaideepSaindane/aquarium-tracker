*Costia* (*Ichthyobodo*), *Chilodonella* and *Trichodina* are external protozoan parasites that, like gill
flukes, are generally invisible without a microscope — there is no spot, coating, or growth to point at.
They present instead as a symptom pattern: flashing and rubbing against surfaces, clamped fins, an excess
or cloudy mucus coat, and sometimes rapid breathing if gills are involved, all while water parameters test
normal.

This overlapping, non-specific symptom picture across several different protozoan species is exactly why
home diagnosis to the exact organism is not realistic, and why presumptive treatment with a broad-spectrum
external anti-protozoal medication — rather than waiting for a definitive identification that most keepers
cannot actually obtain — is the standard, accepted approach once water quality has been ruled out as the
cause.

As with gill flukes, correcting water quality first matters both because poor water can itself cause
similar symptoms and because it is a genuine trigger that lets these otherwise low-level, tolerated
parasite populations bloom into a real problem under stress.
