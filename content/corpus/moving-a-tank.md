immediate_actions:
  - Keep as much of the existing tank water as practical (stored in clean, sealed containers) to reduce the water-chemistry change the fish and biofilter experience at the new location.
  - Keep filter media wet and, ideally, running until close to departure, then keep it damp (not dried out) during transport to preserve as much of the beneficial bacterial colony as possible.
  - Transport fish in secure, well-oxygenated containers (bags with adequate airspace, or aerated containers for a longer trip), minimising total transit time where practical.
  - Set the tank back up at the new location before releasing fish — established decor, substrate, and as much of the original water as was preserved all help the tank feel and function like a continuation rather than a fresh start.
  - Test water parameters closely for the first week or two after a move, since even a well-executed move stresses the biofilter somewhat and a mild parameter fluctuation is common during the settling-in period.
do_not_do:
  - Do not fully drain, dry out, and rinse filter media before a move "to make it easier to transport" — this kills the beneficial bacterial colony the same way a normal filter cleaning mistake would, effectively restarting part of the cycle.
  - Do not assume a short move needs no special care — even a same-day move involves a real, if brief, water chemistry and temperature change plus the stress of handling and confinement.
  - Do not rush fish back into the tank before it has been properly reassembled and, where relevant, allowed to reach a reasonable temperature — a rushed setup adds avoidable additional stress on top of the move itself.

Moving a tank successfully comes down to preserving continuity wherever practical — the same original water,
the same filter media (kept wet, never dried out), the same decor and substrate — rather than treating the
move as an opportunity to start fresh. Each piece preserved reduces the total change the fish and the
biofilter have to absorb at once, and a move that changes everything simultaneously (new water, dried-out
media, rearranged decor) is functionally similar to restarting the tank's cycle from scratch, see
corpus:fishless-cycling and corpus:cycle-crash for what that recovery period actually looks like.

Transit itself should be kept as short and low-stress as practical, with secure, adequately oxygenated
containers for the fish separate from the main tank water and equipment. Reassembling the tank fully at the
destination before releasing fish, rather than releasing them into a still-incomplete setup to save time,
keeps the transition closer to a continuation of their existing environment than a second disruption
immediately after the first.

Even a carefully executed move is worth monitoring closely afterward — testing water parameters for the
following week or two catches any biofilter disruption early, before it becomes the kind of delayed ammonia
or nitrite spike described in corpus:cycle-crash.
