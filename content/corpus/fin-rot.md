Fin rot is one of the most commonly reported fish health issues, and also one of the most commonly
mistreated — it is usually a water-quality symptom wearing the costume of a disease. The fraying, receding
fin edges are typically a secondary bacterial (occasionally fungal) infection taking hold on fin tissue
that has already been weakened by stress, most often from elevated ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, or generally
infrequent water changes.

Treating the visible fin damage with antibacterial medication while leaving the underlying water quality
unaddressed treats the symptom and not the cause, and exposes the tank to the real downsides of
unnecessary antibiotic use — a damaged biofilter and encouraged antibiotic resistance — for a problem that
often resolves with better water changes alone.

The correct sequence is water quality first: correct it, increase change frequency, and watch for a few
days. Fin rot that is mild to moderate and addressed this way commonly stops progressing and the fins
begin to regrow. Medication is the right next step only if that alone does not halt progression, or if the
case is already advanced — reaching toward the fish's body, or accompanied by visible redness or
inflammation at the fin base, which points toward a more serious infection needing more assertive
treatment.
